Paper Discussion Prompts

POLS 081 · SSQL Workshop, Part 1 · Measure → Map → Model

Prompts for small-group discussion of Fransen et al. (2023), Xie et al. (2025) and Tennant, Davies & Tennant (2024).

How this works. In groups of 3–4, discuss each paper for about 4 minutes using the prompts below. Every group gets the same prompts. Then a few groups report back. Jot down one idea per prompt; you don’t need to cover them all.

For every paper, start with the syllabus’s reading questions: What is the question? How do the authors investigate it? What is the most important evidence?

Measure · Fransen et al. (2023)

Taking stock of the implementation gap in climate policy. Nature Climate Change.

The idea. The “implementation gap” has two parts: a policy adoption gap (pledges vs. projected emissions under adopted policies, from Climate Action Tracker) and a policy outcome gap (what adopted policies should achieve vs. what they do achieve). The outcome gap can’t be measured directly, so the authors compare policy-strength scores (CCPI, 2018–2022) with a 2023 outcome index.

Warm-up. Pick one concept: climate ambition, state capacity to implement climate policy, inclusivity of a COP, or vulnerability to climate change. Write down (1) an indicator, (2) a data source, and (3) one thing your indicator misses.

  1. How did the authors get a number for something that can only be measured after the fact?
  2. Validity: does a CCPI policy score capture implementation?
  3. Reliability: CCPI is partly built from expert assessments. Would different experts score the same country the same way?
  4. Where does the outcome gap show up in the data? (Hint: in how countries scatter around the expected diagonal.)

Map · Xie et al. (2025)

Tracing inclusivity at UNFCCC conferences through side events and interest group dynamics. Nature Climate Change.

The idea. UNFCCC records of COP side events, COP9–COP28 (2003–2023): titles, descriptions and host organizations. Topic modelling traces themes; network analysis maps who co-hosts energy events (COP17–28).

  1. What is one observation? An event? An organization? A link between two organizations?
  2. Population or sample? Every registered side event is included. What isn’t? (Unregistered events, country pavilions, the negotiating rooms themselves…)
  3. Measurement: how does “inclusivity” become a number? What would you use?
  4. Variation: what differs across COPs and across actor groups? Is that enough to compare them fairly?
  5. Limits: does a central position in a network mean influence over negotiated outcomes?

Model · Tennant, Davies & Tennant (2024)

Determinants of access to climate finance: Nuanced insights for SIDS and other vulnerable economies. World Development.

The idea. Two stages: do donors use different criteria to decide who is eligible for climate finance than to decide how much each eligible country gets? And do small island developing states (SIDS) get more or less than their vulnerability and adaptive capacity would justify?

  1. Measure: where do the finance numbers come from, and who reports them? (Donors report to the OECD and tag their own projects.)
  2. Is “more than justified by vulnerability” a causal claim or a comparison? What does “justified” assume?
  3. Confounders: what factors does the model hold constant? What could still be missing?
  4. Inference: the data cover nearly every recipient. What do the p-values mean here?
  5. Design: if you wanted a causal answer, what comparison would you look for? (A sudden disaster? An eligibility cutoff?)
